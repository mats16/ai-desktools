# Diagrams and charts (JSON)

Open when a page has a free-form diagram (`frame`), arrows between components, a schedule (`gantt`), a bar chart (`bars`), or a gridded diagram (a comparison table or a matrix). The rest of the page JSON (file header, arrangement, the other components, markup, what stops) is in [pages.md](pages.md). The visual rules for diagrams (patterns, flow direction, logos, cycles, copying versus querying) are §4–§6 of `deck-design` → slides.

## Free-form diagrams and arrows

- **Free-form diagrams:** Only inside a `frame` (which needs `h`), write `x`, `y`, `w` on components (in, from the frame's top-left). Omitting `h` derives it from the content. Use `frame` only for diagrams that `row`, `stack`, and `grid` cannot express (branches, vertically offset boxes, points on an axis). A horizontal flow (A → B → C) is a `row` with `"arrows": true` holding marked cards or `label`s (it uses the full body width, and heights and arrow positions align). Boxes in a `frame` are also placed as `label`s (filled box and text as one shape) or marked cards (`"valign": "middle"` if placing an icon); do not overlay `text` on a filled `box` (it stops when a filled box contains only text; an enclosure holding a heading and cards is allowed). A `frame` laid out as one horizontal row joined by arrows gets a build warning recommending `row`. Components in a `frame` stay where `x` and `y` put them, so a mark (`icon`, `num`, `image`) and the plain `text` to its right placed at the same `y` do not line up: their centers differ by half the difference between the mark's height and the first line's height (a 0.32 in image beside 11pt text sits 0.07 in below the first line). For the heading of an enclosure (a mark, a bold name, and a note on one line), write a `list` with one item (`{"image": …, "text": "**名前**　補足"}`), which centers the mark on the first line. A mark up to 0.5 in tall and the top-aligned plain `text` that starts within 0.5 in to its right get a build warning when their centers differ by more than 0.02 in, with the `y` that aligns them.
- **Arrows:** Give components an `id` and connect them with `{"type": "arrow", "from": "a", "to": "b"}`. If they are apart horizontally, the arrow is drawn horizontally between the facing sides, at the center of their vertical overlap (slides-visual-025. If they do not overlap, diagonally between the side centers). If they are apart vertically, it is drawn vertically. If both ends are boxes (`card`, `code`, `box`, centered `label` and `stat`, centered `text` with fill or outline; `shape` only rectangle or rounded rectangle), the arrow is connected to the centers of the box sides (it follows when the boxes are moved on screen. slides-component-024). `"route": "bent"` makes an elbow line (horizontal–vertical–horizontal if apart horizontally, vertical–horizontal–vertical if apart vertically. For diagrams that branch from or merge into one box. Only when both ends are connectable boxes. A single flow that neither branches nor merges places the boxes in the same row or column and draws straight lines. slides-visual-043 in `deck-design`). For branches, make every branch `bent` (a branch whose center is at the same height is drawn straight without bending. Making only one straight makes it diagonal, and it shows up in the build warnings). To decide the exit and entry sides, write `from` and `to` as `"<id>.<side>"` (side is `top`, `bottom`, `left`, or `right`. `{"from": "k3.right", "to": "he.bottom"}` leaves the right side horizontally and enters the other box's bottom side vertically). Write it on both ends and do not write `route` (only when both ends are connectable boxes). Perpendicular sides bend once (e.g. a line returning from a box in a lower tier to a box in an upper tier); facing sides bend twice (also when joining diagonally separated boxes vertical–horizontal–vertical). The exit side must face the entry point, and the entry side must face the exit point (sides that do not face it stop). Sides facing the same direction draw a U, for a line that returns to an earlier step (`{"from": "back.bottom", "to": "plan.bottom"}`): out of the side, across, and back into the other box's side. It is drawn as three lines that are not connected to the boxes (they do not follow when the boxes are moved on screen), because Google's bent connector cannot be given its depth through the API and a rectangle has one connection point per side. The depth is chosen so that the bottom line stays 0.25 in clear of the components, lines, and labels that lie across its span (between the two ends along the side), and its label does not touch them; inside an enclosure (`box`) holding both ends, only things inside it count, and the space is half the room to its edge when that is less. What lies beside the legs is not avoided by depth (the build warns when a line passes over a component or crosses another line). U lines that share a side are spread along it (the side split into equal parts; when another arrow is connected at the side's center, the U lines toward each direction take that half), and the narrower U is drawn first, so a wider U that holds a narrower one goes around it and its label. The build warns when a U line crosses or overlaps another line (such as two U lines whose ends interleave). Write `"depth"` (in, from the outer of the two sides to the bottom line) only when the chosen depth does not suit the page. The label goes outside the U by default (below the bottom line of a `bottom` U). The build warns when the U or its label leaves the `frame` or the enclosure, and stops when the line would pass over the other end's box (the two boxes overlap in that direction) or the two ends sit at the same point along the side. Ends at `[x, y]`, and ends pointing at images, icons, or layout containers, are not connected. Arrows take no space and are drawn last on the page. Write the line's name (「ログインして要求」 etc.) in `"label"`. It is placed as 11pt `SUB` text: top center for horizontal lines, right of the middle for vertical lines, and on the middle segment for bent lines (the exit segment for lines that bend once) (slides-visual-025). `"label_side"` changes the position (`top` or `bottom` for horizontal lines, `right` or `left` for vertical lines; it stops if it does not match the direction of the segment holding the label). Placing a label as positioned `text` makes it float away from the line or overlap it. `"num"` (an integer from 1 to 99) puts a step number on the line: a small circle (`num` size `xsmall`, 0.22 in, 9pt bold) filled with the line's color (digits in `ON_RED` on `RED`, otherwise the first of `WHITE`, `TEXT`, and `NAVY` that reaches 4.5:1 against the fill, or white or black when none does, so light lines on dark pages stay readable), covering the line there (slides-visual-056 in `deck-design`). The circle goes where it stays 0.03 in clear of every other arrow's line, every round `num` (placed ones and other arrows' circles), and every arrow label, with its own label clear of them and of its own line's other segments; neither the circle nor the label crosses the edge of an enclosure (`box`; inside it is fine) or overlaps any other component, even inside it (touching is fine): the middle of the label's segment (the middle segment of a bent line; the next longest if it is too short) when that is clear, otherwise the middle of the longest clear stretch of that segment, then of the other segments, longest first, never within 0.26 in of a segment's ends. On the last segment of an arrow with a head, "the end" is the base of the arrowhead (0.09 in per pt of `weight` before the tip, 0.11 in at the default 1.25pt; Google draws longer heads on thicker lines), and "the middle" is the middle of the line without the arrowhead, so the circle is as far from the head as from the box it leaves. So on branches and merges, where the part near the shared box overlaps the other branches, the circle lands on the part only that branch passes. With `label_side`, only segments of that direction are tried. With a label, the label goes beside the circle, outside it. When no stretch is clear, the circle stays at the middle and the build warnings say so (and show what passes over it; circles are checked as circles). Write the explanation of each number below the diagram as a `list` with the same numbers (`start` for the second column) with `"size": "xsmall"` and `"fill"` set to the line's color, so the circles match (sample: `samples/json/arrow_steps.json`). When no segment is 0.52 in or longer (plus the arrowhead on the last segment: 0.63 in for a straight 1.25pt arrow, 0.70 in at 2pt), the circle has no room and the build warnings show it. On a `RED` line the white 9pt digits reach only 3.62:1, below the 4.5:1 for bold text under 11pt, and lint lists the pair (the user chose 9pt and accepts this). The circle does not follow when the boxes are moved on screen (like the label). Do not place `num` components by `x`, `y` on lines, because they drift off the line and the position checks read them as components the line passes over. To draw two lines (there and back) between the same two boxes, give each an `"offset"` (in; for horizontal lines down is positive, for vertical lines right is positive) to shift it up/down (left/right) from the box center (e.g. `-0.2` and `0.2`. If the label of the upper line is `top` and that of the lower line is `bottom`, no label sits between the two lines). Offset lines do not attach to the side-center connection points, so they are drawn from side to side without connecting to the boxes (they do not follow when the boxes are moved on screen). It cannot be written on bent lines, ends with a side, or `[x, y]` ends, and it stops if the offset pushes an end outside the box.
- **`arrow` keys:** `from`, `to`, `color`, `head`, `dash`, `weight`, `route`, `label`, `label_side`, `offset`, `num`.
- [slides-pages-038] **Position checks (build warnings):** On the resolved form, it reports: an arrow line passing over other components (excluding the components at both ends and enclosures containing an end; for unfilled text, only where the text is), arrowhead tips gathering within 0.1 in, components partly overlapping (an enclosure fully containing the other, and components placed on the ring of an ellipse (`shape: ellipse`), are fine), labels of diagonal or bent lines overlapping lines, bordered components inside an enclosure (`box`) closer than 0.08 in to its sides (slides-visual-044), bent lines for a single flow that neither branches nor merges (slides-visual-043; arrows with sides written are not checked), and misaligned tier heights among same-shape `stack`s in a `row` (slides-visual-024; `grid` cannot draw arrows and splits columns equally, so a `row` with `"arrows": true` and a `row` with differing column widths are not checked). `slides.py lint` and `dry` show them before building (2026-10-16. In the first JSON deck of 29 pages, 10 of 13 rebuilds of only 1–2 pages included `frame` pages, and half of the image findings in rounds 2–3 of `/deck-review` were `frame` diagrams. 「3 本の矢じりが同じ点に重なる」, found on images in round 3, shows up in this warning from the moment the page is written). Text heights and the bend positions of bent lines (estimated at the middle) are estimates, so check borderline cases on images. Bring this warning to 0 before building `frame` diagrams (do not build and move positions 0.1 in at a time while looking at images).

## Schedules, tables as grids, and charts

- [slides-pages-009] **Build the grid of gridded diagrams (Gantt, comparison tables, matrices) as a table (JSON `table`, or `gantt` for schedules; `d.table` in pages.py).** Do not assemble rectangles and lines. Only things hard to express in a table (bars across months, the today line, labels) are placed as shapes on top of the table. With a table, whoever opens it can easily add rows and edit wording (lint reports `要確認` when there are 3 or more vertical lines with the same top edge and length). Table rows grow to fit the text (`row_h` is the minimum height). For tables with shapes on top, keep cell text to one line and check positions on images.
  - [slides-pages-010] A customer's regular-meeting schedule (one Gantt page) is written with JSON `gantt` (`gantt` below) (the component enforces the numbers below). Body text is 11pt, one month column is 1.0 in, and bar text is 9pt (slides-visual-037. A bar is one shape of fill and text. For bars with tags too, overlaying a separate text frame on a filled rectangle makes lint report `要確認` for text under 11pt); words are up to 5 characters per line and 2 lines, with line breaks set by `\n`. Leftover height below the table (the table's bottom edge goes down to above the footer) is distributed equally to the rows (only as much as keeps rows from looking stretched: about 0.4 in for 11pt body text), and bars are placed at the vertical center of their rows. If only the top is packed and the bottom is empty, the page's visual weight shifts upward. For the next meeting, recreate it in a new folder (step 1 of `deck-workflow`) with page JSON, and get a new URL with `slides.py new`.
- `gantt` (schedule; slides-pages-010): `cols` (column names: `["10 月", "11 月", …]`), `rows` (`[{"head": item, "bars": [{"from": 1, "to": 2, "text": "設計", "status": "tbd"}], "marks": [{"at": 3.5, "text": "本番"}], "note": supplement}, …]`), `today` (red vertical line for today). There is one way of counting: column n runs from position n to n + 1. A bar's `from` and `to` are column numbers (from 1, both ends included; `"from": 1, "to": 2` is all of columns 1–2); the time points `today` and `at` are positions (3 is the left edge of column 3, 3.5 is its middle, the number of `cols` + 1 is the right edge). Bars are in column units; a bar starting mid-month cannot be written. The table is a grid (vertical rules between months), and bars are overlaid as 9pt filled tags (slides-visual-037). Bar text is up to 2 lines (about 5 characters per line; break with `\n`). Overlapping bars move to a lower tier in the same row. Do not write bar colors; write the meaning with `status` (modeled on mermaid's `done` and `crit`; the component decides the colors, so meanings match across materials): `plan` (a fixed plan; default; navy), `tbd` (undecided; dotted outline), `done` (finished; light gray), `hot` (cannot be delayed; red text on a light red background; use for 1–2 bars). State which color means what in the lead or in a legend sentence. `marks` are milestones (mermaid's `milestone`): a red diamond at position `at`, with an 11pt one-line phrase to its right (to its left if it would pass the table's right edge). Do not draw one-day events (start, go-live, deadline) as a one-column bar. If a mark or phrase overlaps a bar, it moves to a lower tier. Writing `note` on any row adds a supplement column on the right, and month columns become 1.0 in (if not written, month columns share the body width). Items and supplements cannot contain markup (nor can milestone phrases). Placed last, directly under the body (`body`), it distributes the leftover height to the rows (up to 0.4 in per row; it does not stretch inside `row` or `stack`). `h` cannot be written. This is `gantt` from past materials
- **Charts:** bar charts are written with JSON `bars` (`bars` below; drawn with shapes, so lint applies). Lines overlaid on bars (numbers with a different unit) and straight lines (targets, limits) can be written with `line` and `threshold` of `bars`. Bars with 2–4 series (grouped side by side, or stacked) are written with `series` and `stack` of `bars`. Lines without bars and charts whose numbers should be editable in Sheets (`createSheetsChart`) have no component, so add one to slides.py the first time it is needed (when past materials were counted on 2026-10-16, pages with boxes sized by values were 29 of 1,780, all single-series bars). Rules for legends, tick marks, and colors are in `deck-design` → slides patterns §5.
- `bars` (bar chart; `deck-design` → slides patterns §5): `items` (`[["2024", 180], …]`; values 0 or more), `unit` (unit after the number), `label` (what the number is, as one bold line centered above the bars: 「通ったタスクの割合」. Leave it out when the title or lead already says it; write it when two charts sit side by side), `max` (the upper end of the scale, for numbers with a fixed full scale such as a rate out of 100 or a score out of 5: `"max": 100` makes 100 the full length, so the room left beyond a bar reads as what is missing. Without it the largest value fills the length, and 91 % looks like all of it. It stops when a value, a stacked total, or the `threshold` is above `max`), `ticks` (`true` draws the value axis: an axis line and gray numbers from 0 at round steps the component picks, below horizontal bars and left of vertical bars [`"max": 100` gives 0, 20, …, 100]. Without `max`, the scale ends at the first round step at or above the largest value or `threshold` (180 → 0, 50, …, 200). With `max`, the last number is `max` when a round step divides it [7 → 0, 1, …, 7]; otherwise the numbers stop before it [99.5 → up to 80], so write a round `max`. Numbers too close to each other are a build warning. No gridlines, because they would cross the numbers at the bar ends. Those numbers already say each value, so write `ticks` only when the reader must see the scale itself, such as where the full score is), `horizontal` (horizontal bars: long names, rankings. Default is vertical bars, for time sequences), `hot` (number of the bar to emphasize: red bar and red number; only one bar. The other bars turn gray [`SUB`], because navy beside red is as strong as the red; they stay navy when a `line` is laid over, because the line is gray), `tentative` (numbers of the bars whose numbers are not final: a period still in progress, an estimate, a forecast. Each is drawn as a dashed outline in the bar's color on a light `CARD` fill, the same look as `tbd` in `gantt`, so "dashed outline = not final" means the same in every component. The number stays in the body color. A bar cannot be both `hot` and `tentative`, because red is for final numbers. One look covers provisional and forecast numbers, so say which one it is in the name or the lead (「10/19 時点」, 「2025（見込み）」, 「Q4 の実績は見込み」), as with the meaning of `gantt` colors). No legend (only `series` below has one) or tick marks (unless `ticks`); the number is written at the end of each bar and one baseline is drawn. The bar numbers and the `threshold` tag share the decimal places of the value with the most (4 and 4.3 → 4.0 and 4.3; 0.6 and 1.03 → 0.60 and 1.03; 100.0 → 100; when that needs 7 or more decimal places, each number keeps its own, because that is float noise from a calculation), so write values as numbers and do not pad them; `line` numbers are written as given. Vertical bar height defaults to 2.6 in (change with `h`); horizontal bars are 0.42 in each (a larger `h` makes the rows taller and the bars thicker in the same ratio). Bars are `NAVY` (gray beside a `hot` bar), and the color cannot be changed (for 2+ series, write `series`). A `line` and a straight `threshold` can be overlaid on the bars (both can be written together. The threshold always uses the bars' scale; a reference line on the line's scale [a target rate etc.] cannot be drawn)
  - `line` (vertical bars only): `{"values": [6.5, 8, 10.5, 13], "unit": "%", "label": "利益率"}`. `values` is one number per bar, in the same order and count as `items` (modeled on mermaid xychart's `line [..]`; names come from `items`). Use it for numbers with a different unit from the bars, such as rates and cumulative totals. In a 0.8 in band above the bars, it draws a gray (`SUB`) line and points on a separate scale from the bars, and writes the number above each point. `label` (what the number is) is one line at the top-left of the band. The band is low, so the scale does not start at 0: the smallest number is at the bottom of the band and the largest at the top (numbers are written at the points). Height defaults to 3.4 in (3.7 in with `label`), and the bar band is the same as without `line`. A point whose number is in `tentative` is hollow, and the segments into it are dashed
  - `threshold`: `{"value": 90, "label": "目標"}`. One number compared on the same scale as the bars, such as a target, limit, or average. It draws a red dotted line and places a tag (「目標 90 分」; unit from `unit`) outside to the right of the line for vertical bars and above the line for horizontal bars. If the number exceeds the bars, the line spans the full height. When the line passes through the number at a bar's end, that number is put inside the bar in white (in the body color on a `tentative` bar, whose fill is light. Outside the line, it would look like a number beyond the line. If the bar is too low or thin to hold it, it is a build warning. lint does not count shapes whose alt-text title is `ai-deck-factory bar` [bars; the description is the name and number] in the space below boxes). There is no component for comparison numbers that differ per bar (plans per year etc.)
  - `series` (2–4 series; vertical bars only): `{"series": ["前年", "計画", "実績"], "items": [["Q1", [237.9, 252.5, 258.1]], …]}`. Write the series names in `series` (one line each), and in `items` write one number per series, in the order of `series` (0 for none; up to 1e15, so write large amounts in a larger unit). Use it when each name has 2+ numbers to compare (plan and actual, last year and this year) or a total made of parts (`stack`). By default the bars of one name are grouped side by side with the number above each bar; `"stack": true` stacks them from the bottom in the order of `series`, writes each number inside its segment, and the total above the bar (when a segment is too low or thin for its number, the number is left out with a build warning). A legend row on top shows each series' color and name, and `unit` is written once at its right end (「単位: 千ドル」), not after each number or on the `threshold` tag (「目標 65」), because repeating it makes the numbers too wide for grouped bars. The component decides the colors (`NAVY`, `OSS`, `GA`, `SUB` in order). `hot`, `line`, and `horizontal` cannot be written with `series`. In `tentative`, a bar number marks every series of that name (a stacked bar for a month in progress); `[name number, series number]` marks one bar (`[[4, 3]]`: only 実績 of the 4th name is an estimate, while 前年 and 計画 are final). `threshold` can be overlaid. Height defaults to 2.9 in (2.6 in plus the legend row). Numbers in grouped bars must fit in one bar's width: write large amounts in a larger unit (「千ドル」, 「億円」) to keep the digits short
