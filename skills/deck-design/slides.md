# Visual: this folder's style, Japanese typesetting, and rules to avoid looking AI-made

Read this before you start writing slides. §1 is this folder's visual rules (the source of truth for colors, positions, and text sizes). §2 onward are the layout rules to follow on top of it. For building with the Slides API (the `slides.py` components), see `build-slides` → writing pages. Where a rule ends with "lint reports it" or "the build warns", this file keeps what to aim for and why; what the check counts, its thresholds, and its exceptions are in `build-slides` → lint (build warnings: → commands; the theme check: → themes).

## 1. This folder's style

[slides-visual-001] This style takes priority over the official skill's design suggestions (pick a palette per deck, white background, no full-width bands, titles at 36pt or larger, and so on). Navy section dividers, a light Oat body surface, red accents. Colors follow Databricks' official brand palette (the 2025 internal template). Text colors must have a contrast of 4.5:1 or more against their background.

[slides-visual-047] **Avoid unnecessary secondary labels and descriptions. Let the layout explain.** Show grouping, order, contrast, and which item is the main one by position, alignment, size, enclosure, and color (§4, §6). Do not add words that only restate what the layout already shows, because they double the text and are the most common default on AI-made pages. Examples: a caption such as 「左: いま　右: これから」 above two columns that already have headings, 「ポイント」 or 「概要」 tags on a box, 「3 つの特長」 above three cards, a description line under each card heading that repeats the heading, and a `sub` under a large number that rephrases its `label`. Before you add a label or a line of supporting text, ask whether a reader would misread the page without it. If not, remove it, and keep it in `authorNotes` if the speaker needs it. `sub` on the cover, section dividers, `stat`, and `band` is optional; fill it only with something the main text does not say. Keep the words the layout cannot carry: the heading, a chart's data name and unit, the source line, release stage tags (§1), the means written on an arrow (slides-visual-036), 「（例）」, and the legend that tells copying from querying (slides-visual-042). slides-visual-008, slides-visual-009, slides-visual-040, and deck-content-055 are cases of this rule.

- The page area is 13.33 × 7.5 in (the AI Desk Tools template; `build-slides` → themes). Left and right margins are 0.6 in.
- [slides-visual-002] **Text is 11pt or larger** (including the cover, section dividers, and source lines). Only the confidentiality label on the same line as the copyright footer and the page number at the bottom right are 9pt, a size that does not get in the way of the body. lint reports it, except the page number, which it does not read; the template and `slides.py` set its size.
- [slides-visual-037] **Supporting text inside a figure (short words placed in a filled shape, such as Gantt bars or labels inside a diagram) is either the same size as the body or 2pt smaller than the body (9pt when the body is 11pt).** At the body size it fills the frame and looks cramped. Going down by only 1pt gives no visible contrast, so if you go smaller, go 2pt smaller. Headings, body text, table cells, and source lines stay at 11pt, and so does text laid over another shape's fill (a card's body, a source line on a background rectangle), because it reads as body text. lint reports it.
- [slides-visual-003] Colors are held as Google Slides theme colors. In the page JSON (pages.py), write the role name (the second column of the table below); the template's theme decides the value (`build-slides` → writing pages and §3). The default theme is `navy` (this folder's colors; named `visual` until 2026-10-31). The other built-in themes are `white` (ink covers and section dividers, warm gray panels, red accents) and `blue` (deep blue covers and section dividers, light blue panels, orange accents). Names in parentheses are the official brand color names.   - [slides-visual-004] When you place groups (such as compute and storage tiers) inside a navy box on a body page, use light boxes (`PANEL`, `CARD`) with `HEAD` text. `NAVY_BLOCK` is for blocks inside a section divider (navy background); on a navy box it differs too little from the background, and the group boundaries do not show. lint reports it as `要確認`.
  - On body pages, heading text is `HEAD`, card background is `CARD`, and card borders and rules are `RULE` (do not write `NAVY` or `WHITE` in either the page JSON or pages.py; they become unreadable in dark body themes. In JSON, `label` and `card` default to `HEAD` and `CARD` when not written).
  - Text on navy is `WHITE`, and notes on navy are `SUB_ON_NAVY`. Text on a `RED` fill is `ON_RED`. Release stage label text is `STAGE_TEXT` for GA, Public Preview, Beta, and Private Preview, and `WHITE` for OSS.

  - Only the red text color `CC2B17` is not in the official palette. The official Lava 600 reaches only 3.4:1 for small text, and Maroon 600 `98102A` does not look like the red accent, so we use Lava 600 made darker (navy). white and blue also darken their `RED` for `RED_TEXT`, just enough to reach 4.5:1 on `PANEL`, the darkest background that red text sits on (`B5332E`: 5.55:1, `C53A00`: 4.83:1).
  - Every role pair of the three built-in themes meets its standard, on the dark section divider they use (`slides.py lint <theme file>`; blue's limits on other dividers are below).
  - [slides-visual-005] Put white text on the red fill `FF3621` only when it is bold and 11pt or larger (3.62:1; slides-visual-039). Red labels in regular weight and 9pt tags use red text (`RED_TEXT`) on the light `FFECE8` background. The S size of the numbered circle inside a figure (0.3 in, 11pt bold; §4) is also 11pt bold, so it reaches 3:1. The one exception is the 9pt number on a red arrow line and its legend (xsmall, slides-visual-019), which the user accepted at 3.62:1.
  - Use Lava 500 `FF5F46` only on navy `1B3139` (4.52:1). On the section divider block `303F47` it reaches only 3.62:1, so use `FFFFFF` or `DCE0E2` there.
  - The official green and yellow lose against the background when used as text color (`00A972` is 3.0:1 on white). Use them only as label fills.
  - [slides-visual-038] The chapter number on a section divider is decoration, but use a color that reaches the large-text standard (3:1) against the divider background. On navy use `DECOR` (`6B8089`, 3.28:1; the earlier `3E5159` was 1.63:1 and hard to read), on red use `NAVY` (3.76:1; earlier it was the same red as the background and invisible), and on white use `DECOR` (3.87:1) (navy's values. blue reaches 3:1 only on its navy divider: `DECOR` `77A8DD` is 2.49:1 on white and `NAVY` is 2.36:1 on its orange `RED`, so a blue theme with white or red section dividers needs other colors). The theme check (`slides.py lint <theme file>`) measures it on each divider background of the theme (2026-10-16).
  - The official font is DM Sans, but it has no Japanese glyphs, so use Noto Sans JP (the "Deliverables" section of `build-slides`). Only code uses the monospace Cascadia Mono (§2). Other companies' themes can also change the fonts (`build-slides` → fonts).

- [slides-visual-050] **The pattern of one page:** kicker (only on pages that need it) → title (one line) → lead (one sentence: what this page says, or a question) → body → conclusion band (only on pages that need it; the main point in one sentence, plus one or two sentences of notes) → source line → footer.

- [slides-visual-051] Body page positions (top edge in inches)

  | Body | 1.78 (1.62 without a lead) | Card headings 12pt bold, body 11pt |

First sentence 13pt bold white, notes 11pt. Inner padding is 0.25 in left and right and 0.2 in top and bottom (larger than the "one character" of §4; matches the original pptx version) |

- [slides-visual-007] **The footer is a copyright notice, not the material's name.** Put the copyright notice (`[brand] copyright` in `desk.toml`; example: `© 2026 Acme, Inc. All rights reserved.`) at the bottom left of the cover, section dividers, and body pages. Keep `｜Internal Only` at the end for internal materials and `｜Confidential` for confidential ones. Do not put a page number on the cover. Match the year to the year of first publication, and do not auto-update the year of existing materials to the current year. Do not use long dashes, and separate after the legal entity name with a period. Pages with `foot=False` that reuse a source as an image as-is keep the original rights notice and credits, and do not get our own notice laid over them. Write sources and credits for third-party material separately from the shared footer.

- [slides-visual-008] **Place the kicker and the conclusion band only on pages that need them.** The same decoration on every page becomes a default unrelated to the subject and looks AI-made (研究 1-8). If the heading states the conclusion, a box below it says the same thing twice (中身 2 節). lint reports decks with too many bands or kickers, and bands that repeat the heading or lead.
  - Place a kicker when it shows the chapter name in a deck with two or more chapters, or when it shows a page role that the layout cannot show (「Appendix」, 「参考」, and so on). Do not place a role the layout already shows, such as 「数字で見る」 above large numbers (slides-visual-047). Do not place a kicker that only rephrases the heading in fewer words. [slides-visual-046] キッカーと地図の行の名前に、作り手の側の言葉（「入口」など）を使わない。章のないはじめのページにはキッカーを付けず、地図では「はじめに」のように聞き手にそのまま通じる言葉にする。
  - Place a conclusion band on pages where the heading is followed by "so what do we do" or "what we need you to decide" (chapter summaries, recommendations, next steps). Do not place one on pages where the heading or lead is already the conclusion. Most body pages can say what they need to say in the lead, so only some pages get one.
  - Pages without one may use the body area down to 6.60. Even without a kicker, do not change the title position (0.62).
  - [slides-visual-009] The same applies to light panel bands placed below the body (one or two lines headed with 「だから」, 「注意」, 「仮説」, and so on). Do not turn into a band what the body can say or what the speaker can say aloud. If bands appear on more than half of the body pages, remove the least needed ones (lint lists the deck).
- [slides-visual-010] **Release stage labels** (colors in the table above). The fill shows whether it is usable in production. GA is green, Public Preview is light green (usable in production), Beta and Private Preview are yellow (not for production; the docs' [release types](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/release-notes/release-types)), and OSS is blue. When building, get them from `STAGE_FILL` in `slides.py` (in JSON, the `label`'s `fill`, with `color` `STAGE_TEXT`, or `WHITE` for OSS). Place the tag inside the box of the element it applies to (to the right of its heading), or to the right of the title if it applies to the whole page. Floating it at an outer corner of a box makes it unclear which box it applies to. lint reports a tag whose word and fill do not match as `要確認`. Usually give features in preview or beta a label. However, in material for a customer with whom the relationship is not yet built, a row of Beta and Preview labels on one page stirs up worry. On that page, hold back the labels, show the release stages in a list in the Appendix, and keep them in `authorNotes` so that the speaker can say them aloud.
- [slides-visual-011] **Give every material a cover.** The same applies to materials used at every regular meeting (schedules, progress lists) (lint reports it as `要確認` when the first page does not have a navy background).
- The cover and chapter section dividers have a navy background. The cover is: small heading (audience and date) 11pt bold `FF5F46` → title 34pt bold white (up to two lines) → subtitle 13pt bold `FF5F46`, with the company name (`[brand] company`) at 11pt at the bottom left. A chapter section divider is: chapter number 88pt bold `6B8089`, chapter title 30pt bold white, subtitle 12.5pt bold `FF5F46`.
- [slides-visual-012] Use a dark section divider to separate chapters. Put the Appendix together after the main part. In decks with more than 10 main pages, place a section divider where the theme changes (without one the change is abrupt). But do not cut it into small pieces. Place dividers per meaningful unit (an audience question or a chapter), and merge a chapter with only one page after its divider into the one before or after (lint reports decks of more than 12 pages without a divider, and one-page chapters). In a deck with dividers, also put a divider before the last group (how to start, decision material, next steps, and so on), and do not mix chapters with and without dividers (check the divider positions on the contact sheet for review aspect 5).
- [slides-visual-040] **Contents and list pages show only item names and page numbers.** Do not add release stage tags, one-line descriptions, or chapter subtitles on the same line (they are on each page; adding them makes every line crowded and hard to read). When there are chapters, list the items in one card per chapter. When there are six or more chapters, or more than 20 items in total, do not list the pages inside the cards; use one line per chapter (chapter number, chapter name, and the divider's page number).

## 2. Text

- [slides-visual-052] **1 行の字数:** 本文は 1 行 15〜25 字。10 字以下で終わる行を作らない。1 つのボックスの本文は 8 行まで。字数は `幅（in）× 72 ÷ 文字サイズ（pt）` で見積もる（全角 1 字と、矢印・丸数字・× は 1 em。数字と小文字はおよそ 0.55 em、大文字は 0.55〜0.75 em だが字で違い、i・l・I は 0.3 em ほど、W・m・M は 0.8〜0.95 em と広い。スペースは 0.22 em。太字は 6〜7% 広い。どれも Noto Sans JP の実測で、DM Sans には別の表があり、ほかの書体も Noto Sans JP の幅で見積もる。字ごとの値は `brand.py` (`build-slides`) の `ASCII_EM` など。lint は組むときと同じ見積もりで、8 行を超える箱を出す）。例: 幅 3.0 in・11pt で 19 字（見た目 1-3）。
- [slides-visual-053] **Line spacing:** Set the line spacing of multi-line body text (`spacing` of JSON `text`, or of `d.text` in pages.py, in %) to 125–135. Japanese line gaps are generally 0.5–1.0 em, wider for longer lines (W3C Requirements for Japanese Text Layout). Two-line headings use 110. Do not set it on one-line frames (見た目 1-2). Line advance in Google Slides has not been measured. If it looks cramped in the image, widen it.
- [slides-visual-013] **改行:** 最終行は句読点を入れて 3 字以上にする。単語や文節の途中で折り返さない。「のは」「ので」「には」「によって」は前の行に残す。数字と単位（「1.56 em」「10 倍」）を 2 行に割らない。太字や括弧で強調した語を 2 行に割らない。全角の開き括弧（「（」「「」）の前で手で改行しない（行頭に来ると半字下がって見え、左端がそろわない）。直すときは、文を短くするか、ボックスの幅を変える（見た目 1-4）。Google Slides は和文を 1 字ずつ折り返すので、自動の折り返しが文節の途中に入る見込みを lint が出す（slides-lint-024。見積もりなので、画像で確かめる）。
- **和欧間:** 半角スペースを入れる（`deck-writing` の「書き方」）。ただし、句読点と閉じ括弧の前、開き括弧の後ろには入れない。和文の中の括弧は全角にする。中黒「・」の前後には入れない（見た目 1-10）。lint が数える。
- [slides-visual-014] **Code uses a monospace font. The default is Cascadia Mono, and the theme can change it (`mono`; `build-slides` → fonts).** Make the alphanumerics monospace in code boxes and in tags and table cells that hold only a command (a column of a command list). Command names inside Japanese and running text stay in Noto Sans JP. Cascadia Mono is the default font of Windows Terminal, is on Google Fonts, and has no ligatures. Fonts with ligatures (JetBrains Mono, Fira Code, Cascadia Code) turn `->` into one arrow glyph, and a reader copying the code may type `→`, so `slides.py` stops them in the theme's `mono` (the research history is in `Research/monospace_font_google_slides_20261009.md`). Monospace fonts have no Japanese glyphs, so do not set Japanese text in monospace (`"mono": true` on JSON `text` and `label`, and `"mono_cols": [1]` on `table` 〔columns counted from 1〕, make only the ASCII runs monospace). Write code boxes with JSON `code`, not copied old components, which do not become monospace. lint reports Japanese in monospace, fonts outside the theme, and code on navy that is not monospace.
- **Bold:** Use it only for headings and important words. At most one or two places per box. Do not use italics (Japanese has no italics). Underline only links (見た目 1-11).
- [slides-visual-015] **Emphasizing numbers:** Make the number large and the unit one step smaller (example: number 32pt, unit 16pt). For materials meant to be read, 32–40pt is enough for large numbers (見た目 1-11・2 節). Do not drop the unit and place only the number (「16 モデル」, not 「16」). Put the number and unit in one text frame so their baselines align, and write what the number counts briefly right below it. Do not use the pattern of a column of numbers with descriptions of uneven line counts beside them (the vertical center of each description shifts row by row and looks jagged). For a breakdown (55 = 16 + 39), put each number at the head of a card per part, and state the total in the lead. lint reports large paragraphs that contain only a number as `要確認`.
- [slides-visual-016] **Choose alignment by role.** As a baseline, left-align descriptive text, and center short step names, diagram nodes, and groups of numbers. In cards, arrange meaningful icons and text as a unit and keep the padding even. Do not let a component's default left alignment flow to every element. When porting, check the alignment of the source material. **When you line up peer elements, use the same alignment within them.** Choose left or center by the roles above, and do not left-align everything. But within peer boxes laid out side by side (boxes that each hold an icon, heading, and description, as in 「注文 → 中で回る → 出てくるもの」), do not give only one box a different alignment (it alone looks unnatural). Check this in review aspect 5.
- [slides-visual-017] **Do not fill the heading above a code box.** The navy code box already acts as an enclosure, so make the heading bold text, not a tag or band (a navy tag looks doubled with the box). Give the headings of side-by-side columns the same form, and do not change the fill of only one. To emphasize one column, change the whole column box (for example, make the Lakebase side navy in a comparison diagram), or show it with the words of the conclusion band. lint reports a filled tag or band placed right above a code box. Check this in review aspect 5.

## 3. Color

- Choose colors from the table in §1. What to emphasize, how categories are shown, and the color practices (at most four colors, not color alone, color pairs, surfaces) are in `SKILL.md`.
- [slides-visual-039] Text-to-background contrast is 4.5:1 or more for regular text under 18pt and bold text under 11pt, and 3:1 or more for text at 18pt or larger or bold text at 11pt or larger. The bold threshold was lowered from WCAG 2's 14pt to the body size of 11pt (bold body text is readable at 3:1. The user decided this on 2026-10-16. It differs from WCAG 2 AA for bold text at 11–14pt). The colors in the §1 table meet this in the pairs listed. When you use a pair not in the table, look at lint's 「コントラストが基準に届かない色の組」.

## 4. Shapes

- [slides-visual-018] Cards, process nodes, and filled labels default to subtle rounded corners with a radius of **0.1 in** (the roundness of the original AI Deck Factory pptx). Do not increase the radius for larger boxes (pills, below, are the one exception). `slides.py` builds this roundness by combining rectangles and circles. Do not use the roundness of Google's standard `ROUND_RECTANGLE` as is. For backgrounds, image frames, and other things that need square corners, write `"shape": "rectangle"` on JSON `box` and `text`. On JSON `card`, write `"corner": "square"` for square corners and `"corner": "pill"` for pills (half circles at both ends). Use them only for a reason: tracing the shapes of a source material, or telling one kind of box apart from the others in a figure (such as pills for services and square boxes for data layers). Use `pill` on short cards of one or two lines; on a tall card the half circles get large and eat into the text (the build warns). Inside meaningful line icons, you may combine whatever shapes you need.
- [slides-visual-019] Choose numbered circles from four sizes: xsmall 0.22 in, 9pt (numbers on arrows and their legend, slides-visual-056; 9pt is the floor for text in a filled shape, slides-visual-037. On a red line the white 9pt digits reach only 3.62:1, below slides-visual-005; the user chose 9pt and accepts this, 2026-11-04 in `docs/decisions.md`); small 0.3 in, 11pt (markers inside a figure); medium 0.36 in, 14pt (cards and steps; the default); large 0.48 in, 18pt (large stages). For medium and large, a digit height of about 40% of the circle looks balanced. Small uses 11pt to match the surrounding 11pt text (it is 11pt bold, so white on red reaches 3.62:1; slides-visual-039). With a small circle and large text, "10" spills out of the circle (write `xsmall`, `small`, `medium`, or `large` in `"size"` of JSON `num`; `NUM` in `slides.py`).
- [slides-visual-020] **Put number badges only on things whose order has meaning (steps, stages, numbers referred to by page).** If you put icons (product icons, line art) on cards for peer items, remove the numbers and keep only the icons. With both a number and an icon, two markers line up at the head of the row. `slides.py` stops a JSON card or list item with both; whether an order has meaning is checked in review.
- [slides-visual-021] **Arrows that pass between rows of boxes, such as a timeline axis, are at least 0.2 in away from the boxes above and below.** If they are close enough to touch the box edges, the arrow looks like a box border and is hard to read. Draw the axis midway between the boxes above and below. lint reports it.
- [slides-visual-022] **A small heading that moves to a different topic within one page (such as 「いまの〇〇では」 below a figure) is shown as separate from the figure above.** Put a thin rule (`RULE`) or a gap of 0.3 in or more between it and the figure above, and make the small heading at least 3pt larger than the bold labels inside the figure (such as the words under 「比べる軸」). With the same color and weight and only 1pt of difference, it reads as a continuation of the figure above. Say what the section is about in the small heading itself; do not add a supporting phrase beside it (slides-visual-047). Even if the small heading's color or weight differs from the figure above, a narrow gap still makes it look like a continuation, so apply the same rule. You do not need a large gap; a rule with 0.1–0.2 in above and below is enough.
- [slides-visual-023] When you enclose a range for emphasis, use a red (`RED`) rounded rectangle with a 2pt line and the same 0.1 in corner radius as the cards inside. The thickness is the same for dashed lines (a range inside a figure; `{"type": "box", "line": "RED", "shape": "round_rectangle", "dash": true}`) and solid lines (one table column; do not write `"dash"`). `box` makes red lines 2pt by default (other borders are 0.75pt), and lint reports thinner red outlines and Google's default radius. The examples are on the template's component pages (pages 4 and 5).
- [slides-visual-024] **When you line up columns, align the top and bottom edges of the boxes across columns.** If only one column has a small heading (such as 「MCP の 3 種類」), the other column's box starts at the small heading's height, and both top and bottom look misaligned. Put the small heading in both columns or in neither. Fit box heights to the content, and make the total of the boxes in one column match the height of the boxes in the other columns. For columns of the same form (for example, three columns each stacking a card and a code box), make the box heights equal per tier, so that the top edges of the lower tier are on one horizontal line: in JSON, put them in a `grid` (`cols` set to the number of columns) in tier order, not `stack`s in a `row`, because `grid` equalizes heights per row. For one- or two-line text, first equalize the line counts by the line break positions and the wording (stop the parenthetical line break that drops just one word to the next line). lint and the build report misaligned columns; for columns of the same form, fix them.
- [slides-visual-025] Draw a horizontal arrow between two boxes at the vertical center of the boxes on both sides. Put the words for the arrow above the arrow, and do not shift the arrow to make room for the words. If you shift it, it looks misaligned in LibreOffice, PowerPoint, and Google Slides alike (lint reports it). However, when you draw two arrows, going and returning, between the same two boxes, shift the two equally above and below the center, and put the words above the upper line and below the lower line (on JSON pages, the arrow's `offset` and `label_side`). When there is no room above, you may put the words below the line (to the left for a vertical line) (check on the image).
- No shadows. No gradients. The official skill recommends shadows, but in Japanese materials they are said to be a sign of AI-made work (見た目 1-9、AI 実践 1-5).
- [slides-visual-027] Leave one character's width between the frame and the text. Do not pack text up to the edge of the frame. Rather than shrinking the text to fit the frame, cut the text or enlarge the frame (見た目 1-8). For left- or right-aligned text in a filled box, the JSON components leave one character's width by default (`pad` in `build-slides` → writing pages; for pages.py, `d.text` and `d.label` in `build-slides` → older decks "Laying out shapes and text"). The same applies top and bottom. The bottom especially: adding bullets or items moves the last line toward the bottom edge of the frame, so when you add content also fix the box height, so that the gap between the last line and the bottom edge is about the same as the top padding. The same applies to center-aligned bands and panels (such as a two-line note in a light band): do not make the box height just the line count. If it becomes two lines, either rephrase it to fit on one line, or add one character's width above and below. For white cards, keep the gap between the last line and the bottom edge at 0.28 in (about two characters' width) or more, because even with one character's width the third bullet line looks close to the card's bottom edge (a one-line tag right above the bottom edge, such as 「2 回まで」, needs only one character's width). lint reports the left or right gap of left- or right-aligned text, and `gslides.py check` measures on the image Google drew, because line heights cannot be estimated from the JSON: the bottom gap of top-aligned text of 2 or more lines (and of a 1-line tag in a white card), and both gaps of center-aligned text of 2 or more lines, all inside a filled box. Other text (one line, or over no fill) is not measured, so check it on the image.
- [slides-visual-028] Text in a standalone light panel (a filled box such as `PANEL` with text in it, with no box of the same height beside it) is centered vertically (`"valign": "middle"` is the default for filled JSON `text`), and the height fits the content. If you leave it top-aligned and make the box larger, only the bottom is empty, and its alignment does not match panels centered on other pages. Panels in the same row also use the same alignment. Columns of cards of the same height side by side may stay top-aligned so their headings line up. `gslides.py check` reports top-aligned panels left empty below, on the image.
- [slides-visual-044] **Place components inside an enclosure (the border of a `box`) at least 0.08 in inside all four sides of the enclosure.** Do not give a component's edge the same coordinate as the enclosure's edge (with a bordered enclosure the lines overlap and look like one, with a filled enclosure the component looks stuck to the edge, and in both cases the enclosure cannot be read). 0.08 in is the lower limit that stops lines that overlap or nearly touch; usually leave about 0.15 in (one 11pt character's width). If the width is not enough, make the inner components narrower or tighten the gaps. The build warns.
